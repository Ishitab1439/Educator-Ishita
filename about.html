My philosophy

I start from what students already know and move gradually towards the unknown, so that every learner can begin, whatever their prior knowledge. I open units with open questions, sticky notes and student voice before I explain anything, because their ideas keep teaching me more than I expect. Teaching is a two-way street, and I want every student to leave knowing they have something to contribute.

I believe learning is strongest when it is connected and practised. A student who uses the nets from Maths to plan a wood cut, or who debugs a robot and then writes advice to their past self, is learning how to think, not only what to think. So I build in explicit support for research and inquiry, make reflection a regular habit and let students make real things.

I lead the same way I teach: by learning alongside people. I run professional development for colleagues, share what I try in my journal, and keep stepping into unfamiliar roles, from the operations side of IGNITE to mentoring innovators, because I learn most by doing.

How I use AI and tools

I use AI and automation to save time on planning and admin: drafting unit and lesson outlines, building templates and organising my day. The thinking stays mine. I write and check every task, rubric and piece of feedback by hand, and I read everything a tool produces before it reaches a student or a colleague.

I keep student data out of these tools. I do not enter names, grades or personal details, and I judge student work myself against the criteria. I also teach students to treat AI as a thinking partner and to be open about when and how they used it.
