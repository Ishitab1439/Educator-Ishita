From idea to object

Drag the slider to follow one of my projects, the IGNITE 2026-27 name tag, from sketch to CAD to the finished piece.

Sketch

The brief was an IGNITE name tag with the mascot, the event and a name on it. I planned the layout first: fox mascot on the left, event name and student name on the right, and a hole for a lanyard. I also marked what would be engraved and what would be cut.

CAD

I turned the plan into a vector file for the laser cutter. Red lines cut the rounded outline and the lanyard hole. Black lines engrave the fox and the text, and the student name is swapped in for each tag.

Built

The tag is engraved and cut from clear acrylic on a CO2 laser. Looking at the result, I would leave more margin around the tagline next time so it does not run into the edge.

What I can make

The areas I design and build in, as I build towards DP Design Technology.

CAD modelling

I model in Autodesk Fusion 360, sketching, constraining, extruding, joining and rendering objects in 3D, and I look at each model from every angle before anything is made. In November 2025 I completed the Advanced CAD Designing course, and I wrote about what it taught me in Learning by designing. Stronger CAD lets me guide students in building accurate digital models, testing prototype iterations and working the way professional designers do.

Tools: Autodesk Fusion 360

3D printing

I completed the three-credit course "3D Printing and Design for Educators" from NITTTR Bhopal on SWAYAM, which opened up the physics, chemistry, mathematics and materials science behind the process. What I take into class is the link between a digital model and a physical part: why orientation, layer height and infill change strength, finish and print time, and why a first print is rarely the last.

Tools: Autodesk Fusion 360 for modelling

Laser cutting

I trained on a CO2 laser cutting machine in a one-day programme at the Atal Community Innovation Centre at CBIT, and I am bringing the process into my design lessons. Laser cutting rewards a precise two-dimensional drawing, so it connects sketching, measuring and tolerance to something a student can hold in minutes, and it makes iteration fast and cheap.

The tag in the photo is an IGNITE 2026-27 name tag: the fox mascot, event name and tagline engraved on clear acrylic, with the outline cut and a hole for a lanyard. The second image is the vector file on the machine software, where red lines cut through the sheet and black lines engrave the surface.

Tools: CO2 laser cutting machine, acrylic sheet, vector design software

IoT and electronic systems

In my Design classes I teach students to build and simulate Arduino systems that sense something, decide and respond. The circuit shown is a student's simulation: a potentiometer read on analog pin A0 controls an LED, wired through a resistor to digital pin 13. Students start with this input, process and output pattern, then move on to real sensors such as a soil moisture sensor and an OLED display. Simulating first lets them find wiring and code mistakes before touching real components.

See students' prototypes and simulations

Tools I teach with: Arduino Uno, breadboard, potentiometer, LED, soil moisture sensor, OLED display, circuit simulator

Products using wood

I completed a one-month Hands-on Practical Training Programme in Carpentry and Workshop Practice at the CBIT Central Workshop, in six Saturday sessions from 8 August to 19 September 2026. I learned safe use and care of hand tools such as jack planes, steel rules, mortise chisels and marking gauges, measuring and marking out, planing timber to size, cutting grooves and chiselling, and making the half lap, dovetail and bridle joints. Working in wood by hand shows students how a drawing becomes a precise, buildable product.

Tools: jack plane, mortise chisel, marking gauge, steel rule
