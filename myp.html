The design cycle, criteria A to D

The MYP Design cycle has four criteria. Tap one to see what students do in it, and the tasks, templates and samples that go with it.

  • Students find a real problem, research it and justify why it needs solving. They plan their research, analyse existing products and finish with a design brief.
  • Students turn research into a design specification, sketch a range of feasible ideas, choose one and justify the choice, then draw planning diagrams to build from.
  • Students plan the making step by step, learn and show the technical skills they need, follow the plan to build the solution and justify any changes along the way.
  • Students design tests for their solution, explain how well it worked, say how it could be improved and consider its impact on the people and context it was made for.

Use the arrow keys to move between stages if you are on a keyboard.

Criterion A: Inquiring and analysing

Students investigate a real problem and build the evidence for what to design.

What students do

  1. Explain and justify the need for a solution
  2. Construct a detailed research plan
  3. Analyse a range of existing products that inspire a solution
  4. Develop a detailed design brief

Criterion B: Developing ideas

Students turn their research into a specification and a design they can build.

What students do

  1. Develop design specifications with clear success criteria
  2. Present a range of feasible design ideas
  3. Present the chosen design and the reasons for choosing it
  4. Develop accurate planning drawings and diagrams

Criterion C: Creating the solution

Students plan the making and build the solution, recording the process as they go.

What students do

  1. Construct a logical plan that a peer could follow
  2. Demonstrate excellent technical skills
  3. Follow the plan to create the solution
  4. Justify changes made to the design and the plan

Criterion D: Evaluating

Students test the solution and decide what it achieved and what should change.

What students do

  1. Design detailed testing methods to measure success
  2. Explain the success of the solution against the specification
  3. Explain how the solution could be improved
  4. Explain the impact of the solution on the client or target audience
