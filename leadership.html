Supporting teachers on ManageBac

Training colleagues to plan, teach and report in one system, and carrying it across academic years.
Context
Our school uses ManageBac for planning, teaching and learning, and reporting. A platform only helps when teachers can use it confidently in their daily work, and it has to move cleanly from one academic year to the next without losing the work already done.
My role
ManageBac Implementation and Support Lead. I am the person colleagues turn to for training and help, and I own two recurring responsibilities: the academic year transition and reporting.
What I did
Professional development. I planned and ran sessions that showed teachers how to use ManageBac to organise their teaching and learning, so that planning, student work and records sit in one place.
Everyday support. I help teachers when they are stuck, so that small problems do not become reasons to avoid the platform.
Academic year transition. I carried ManageBac over into the new academic year, so teachers could start the year with their classes and structures ready.
Reporting. I manage reporting, so that reports are produced from the same system teachers already use for their teaching.

MYP 3 Grade Lead: a student-led assembly

Supporting students to plan and deliver "Well-being Through Self-Expression", from first idea to the stage.
Context
MYP 3 students led a school assembly on the theme "Well-being Through Self-Expression", taking ownership from planning to execution.
My role
MYP 3 Grade Lead. I supported the students through the process, working alongside their teachers, while the students made the decisions.
What I did
I guided and encouraged the students as they planned, prepared and ran the assembly themselves. My job was to give them the space to lead and to back them as they stepped into roles as leaders, creators, collaborators and decision-makers.
Result
The assembly became a celebration of student voice, talent and ownership, and a clear example of student agency in action: students can achieve a great deal when they are given the space to lead.

Pastoral care leadership: a proactive system

Planning social-emotional learning, mentoring homeroom teachers and working with parents, before problems arise.
Context
Pastoral care can easily become reactive, with action taken only once a problem has already appeared. As MYP 3 Grade Lead, I wanted students' wellbeing to be looked after earlier than that.
My role
MYP 3 Grade Lead, leading pastoral care for the grade.
What I did
Planning social-emotional learning (SEL). I plan the SEL programme for the grade, so that wellbeing is taught on purpose and not only discussed when something goes wrong.
Mentoring homeroom teachers. I mentor the homeroom teachers on pastoral care, so that they feel supported and confident in handling it.
Coordinating with parents. I coordinate with parents, so that school and home stay connected about how students are doing.
A three-tier discipline system. I set up a three-tier system for discipline, so that concerns are handled in clear, graded steps and not case by case after the fact.
What proactive looks like
Teaching skills before they are needed. In my "Balance in my life" activity, students rate the areas of their lives, draw balance sheets and trees, and ask which part feels unbalanced and what they can do to restore it. They practise self-management when things are going well, not only in a crisis. Read the reflection.
Giving students language for boundaries. The "Uncover your boundaries" worksheet helps students say what they are allowed to do, what makes them uncomfortable and what new rules they want to set, so they can recognise when to say no. Read the reflection.
Giving wellbeing a student voice. The MYP 3 assembly on "Well-being Through Self-Expression" was planned and led by students, so wellbeing was something they owned and not something done to them.
A system with clear tiers. The three-tier discipline system sets out in advance how concerns are handled at each level, so students, homeroom teachers and parents know what to expect and support can start early.
Building belonging early. As a homeroom teacher I had students co-create a "Faces of MYP 1" wall, so the room reflected them before any difficulty arose. Read the reflection.
Result
I replaced a system that relied on reacting to problems with a proactive one, where planning, teacher support and communication with parents are already in place.

How I lead

Tap a principle to see it in action.

Build systems, not heroics

On ManageBac I do not just answer questions. I ran professional development so teachers can organise their own teaching and learning in the system, and I carry the academic year transition and reporting through the same system, so the work does not depend on me being in the room.

Start with the learner's problem

In Criterion A, my students knew products well but stayed at the surface when asked about ingredient function or ethical sourcing. So I slowed the design cycle down and taught research and inquiry skills with curated reading before any ideas or tools came in.

Train, then check it stuck

After a ManageBac session I keep supporting teachers when they get stuck, so small problems do not stop them using the system. Training once is not enough; it has to hold in everyday teaching.

Show the work

I share what I try, in my journal and on this site: task plans, templates and student examples. When I led a session on student voice, choice and ownership, colleagues co-designed their own units in the room.

Say what did not work

When I introduced exit tickets with metacognitive prompts in a design project, I learned that doing it once or twice does not change much. Real impact comes only when reflection becomes a regular habit in the classroom, so I now treat it as routine and not a one-off activity.
