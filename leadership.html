Context
MYP 3 is one grade of the Middle Years Programme at Sreenidhi International School.
My role
Grade Level Coordinator for MYP 3, and a member of the Supportive IB Leadership Team.
What I did
I built a proactive pastoral system and not a reactive one: planned SEL, mentored homeroom teachers, coordinated with parents and set up a three-tier discipline system. I also oversee interdisciplinary learning and joint planning across subject groups.
The three tiers
Tier 1: Classroom. The subject or homeroom teacher has a calm, private conversation with the student, reminds them of the agreed expectations and helps them put things right. Most concerns end here.
Tier 2: Homeroom teacher and parents. If it continues, the homeroom teacher meets the student, agrees clear next steps with them and informs the parents, so school and home give the same message.
Tier 3: Grade Lead. For serious or repeated concerns I step in, meet the student and parents, and agree a support plan, with the wider school team where needed.
What proactive looks like
Teaching skills before they are needed. In my "Balance in my life" activity, students rate the areas of their lives, draw balance sheets and trees, and ask which part feels unbalanced and what they can do to restore it. They practise self-management when things are going well, not only in a crisis. Read the reflection.
Giving students language for boundaries. The "Uncover your boundaries" worksheet helps students say what they are allowed to do, what makes them uncomfortable and what new rules they want to set, so they can recognise when to say no. Read the reflection.
Giving wellbeing a student voice. The MYP 3 assembly on "Well-being Through Self-Expression" was planned and led by students, so wellbeing was something they owned and not something done to them.
A system with clear tiers. The three-tier discipline system sets out in advance how concerns are handled at each level, so students, homeroom teachers and parents know what to expect and support can start early.
Building belonging early. As a homeroom teacher I had students co-create a "Faces of MYP 1" wall, so the room reflected them before any difficulty arose. Read the reflection.
