Learner Profile board game

A game board built around the IB Learner Profile, with question and card spaces from Start to End.

MYP Design students, Learning Fest
Brief
Create a new game board centred on the IB Learner Profile.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What are the Learner Profile attributes, and how could each become a question, a card or a move in a game?
Conceptual: How can a circuit make a board game interactive? How can a game make a value such as being caring or principled visible?
Debatable: Is a game a better way to learn about character than a discussion?
Process
After an electrical board games unit, students chose to design an entirely new board on the Learner Profile. They laid out a path from Start to End with question, card and battle spaces, placed the attributes around the edges and wired it up. They reused what they had learned without being prompted.
Skills and criteria
Transfer of learning. ATL creative thinking and self-management skills. Criteria B, C and D: planning the layout, making the board and testing it. Read the story.
What students found
Circuit planning, user interaction, iteration and testing carried over into a new context.
Next inquiry
What would make the game work for players who have never met the Learner Profile?

Circuit behind the game board

Wires, tape and a battery on a cardboard base, planned to light up the board.

MYP Design students, Learning Fest
Brief
Make the board interactive with a working circuit.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What does a circuit need to complete a path for current?
Conceptual: How does the plan of the wiring decide what a player experiences? Why test each connection as you build?
Debatable: Is a hidden circuit part of the design, or only a tool behind it?
Process
Students planned the wiring on a cardboard base, connected a battery and the wires with tape, and tested the connections themselves.
Skills and criteria
ATL critical thinking and self-management skills. Criteria C (creating the solution) and D (evaluating). Read the story.
What students found
The circuit plan is as much a design as the board on the front.
Next inquiry
How could the wiring be tidied so that it is easier to test and repair?

Lip balm research boards

A hand-drawn mind map, ingredient research and reflection for a lip balm unit.

MYP Design students, Criterion A
Brief
Analyse the need for a product by researching ingredients, environmental impact and ethical sourcing.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What is in a lip balm, and what does each ingredient do?
Conceptual: How do user appeal, sustainability and responsibility pull against each other in a product? How does knowing an ingredient's source change what we think of it?
Debatable: Should a product be judged on how well it works or on how it is made?
Process
Part of the MYP 1 unit Sustainable Glam (key concept: development; global context: globalization and sustainability). In Lesson 1 the cycle was slowed down: students worked with curated readings and were taught how to skim for relevance, scan for critical information and tell description from implication. They then turned their notes into running notes, graphic organisers, a hand-drawn mind map, sticky notes and a written reflection.
Skills and criteria
ATL research and thinking skills. Criterion A: Inquiring and Analysing. Read the reflection.
What students found
Students moved from what they know about products to analysing function, impact and sourcing, and began to link materials, markets and responsibility.
Next inquiry
How can what they found shape the specification for their own product in Criterion B?

Line-following robots of the future

Four panels: delivery robots, transport pods, a trash-sensing robot and a robot with cameras.

Student sketch, design lesson
Brief
Draw your vision of the future and show how line-following robots can solve real-world problems.
Inquiry questions
Factual: Where do line-following robots already work today?
Conceptual: How can following a line help a robot do a job? What problem would you choose, and why?
Debatable: Should robots take over jobs that people do?
Process
Students sketched freely in a lesson designed by Chaitra R, after learning how a line-following robot senses and follows a path. They drew a problem, the setting and the robot's job.

Butterfly of prints

Rainbow colours printed with cut vegetables inside a drawn butterfly outline.

Grade 7 student, maths through art
Brief
Learn how cutting a solid at different angles changes its cross-section, ahead of the volume of prisms.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What shapes appear when a vegetable is cut straight across, and when it is cut at an angle?
Conceptual: How does the angle of a cut change the shape of the cross-section? How is a cross-section connected to the volume of a prism?
Debatable: Is a print a mathematical model or a piece of art, or both?
Process
Students first predicted the shapes, then cut vegetables at different angles, painted the cut faces and printed what they found inside a drawn outline. They compared prints with a partner and explained why two cuts gave different shapes.
Skills and criteria
ATL thinking skills (predicting, transfer) and communication skills (explaining a result). Mathematical links: cross-sections, 3D shapes and prisms.
What students found
The shape changed with the angle of the cut, and each picture was different, so each student's perspective showed.
Next inquiry
If a prism is a stack of identical cross-sections, how could we use the printed shape to find its volume?

Gold-shade lamp

A finished lamp with a gold textured shade, a wooden stem and a teal base.

MYP Design class, product
Brief
Design a functional lamp from scratch, following the IB Design Cycle.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What does a lamp need to do, and who will use it?
Conceptual: How do form, materials and function affect each other in a lamp? How does a student's own idea shape the design?
Debatable: Should a lamp be judged more on how it works or on how it looks?
Process
Students followed the design cycle: they inquired into what a lamp needs to do, brainstormed ideas, sketched and chose a design, then made and finished their own lamp. Each student chose their materials and features, from the shade to the base.
Skills and criteria
Criteria A to D of MYP Design: inquiring and analysing, developing ideas, creating the solution and evaluating. ATL creative thinking and self-management skills.
What students found
Starting from student ideas led to lamps no two alike, and students owned their learning.
Next inquiry
How could the same lamp be redesigned for a different user or a different material, and what would change?

Sprint team and prototype

A team shares a cardboard prototype and a poster of their plan.

MYP students, Rapid Prototyping Sprint
Brief
Work in a team to ideate, prototype and pitch a solution to a real-world problem in one day.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What problem will your team solve, and who has it?
Conceptual: Why does a quick, low-fidelity prototype help us learn before we build the real thing? How do ideas change when a team tests them early?
Debatable: Is a good pitch more about the idea or about how it is told?
Process
Teams moved from a real-world problem to ideas, a low-fidelity prototype and a pitch within a single day, working with simple materials and a poster.
Skills and criteria
ATL collaboration, communication and creative thinking skills. Criteria B (developing ideas) and C (creating the solution), with a brief evaluation in the pitch. Read the sprint story.
What students found
A strong idea can be tested early with simple materials.
Next inquiry
What would the team test next, and with whom, to move from a rough model to something people could use?

The Thinking Wall

Students reflect on their thinking style and invent a "fifth thinker".

MYP students, thinking skills
Brief
Read a story about four thinkers and reflect on how you think.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What are the four kinds of thinker in the story, and how does each solve a problem?
Conceptual: How can different thinkers help a team? What would a "fifth thinker" add?
Debatable: Is there a right kind of thinking for every situation?
Process
Students read "The Four Thinkers and the Forgotten Science Fair", then answered prompts on a shared board in three stages: discovering their thinking style, working together, and stretching their thinking by inventing a fifth thinker. They finished with a discussion of patterns in how the class thinks. Read the story.
Skills and criteria
ATL thinking and reflection skills; collaboration.
What students found
Different kinds of thinkers can help each other.
Next inquiry
How will I use all four kinds of thinking in my next school challenge?

Balance worksheet and see-saw

A student rates areas of life, draws the tangle and shows an unbalanced see-saw.

MYP student, Balanced and self-management
Brief
Explore what it means to be balanced, assess areas of your life and show it visually.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What are the areas of my life, and how would I rate each one?
Conceptual: What does balance mean for me? How can a picture, such as a wheel, a see-saw or a tree, show how balanced I am?
Debatable: Is a perfectly balanced life possible or even desirable?
Process
Students defined balance in their own words, rated areas of life with reasons, and showed the result visually. They then wrote about what they wanted to change.
Skills and criteria
Learner Profile attribute: Balanced. ATL self-management and reflection skills, and communication through a visual.
What students found
Rest and sleep were out of balance, and an early sleep was the first step.
Next inquiry
What small step will I try for a week, and how will I check whether it made a difference?
