Learner Profile board game

A game board built around the IB Learner Profile, with question and card spaces from Start to End.

MYP Design students, Learning Fest
Brief
Create a new game board centred on the IB Learner Profile.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What are the Learner Profile attributes, and how could each become a question, a card or a move in a game?
Conceptual: How can a circuit make a board game interactive? How can a game make a value such as being caring or principled visible?
Debatable: Is a game a better way to learn about character than a discussion?
Process
After an electrical board games unit, students chose to design an entirely new board on the Learner Profile. They laid out a path from Start to End with question, card and battle spaces, placed the attributes around the edges and wired it up. They reused what they had learned without being prompted.
Skills and criteria
Transfer of learning. ATL creative thinking and self-management skills. Criteria B, C and D: planning the layout, making the board and testing it. Read the story.
What students found
Circuit planning, user interaction, iteration and testing carried over into a new context.
Next inquiry
What would make the game work for players who have never met the Learner Profile?

Lip balm research boards

A hand-drawn mind map, ingredient research and reflection for a lip balm unit.

MYP Design students, Criterion A
Brief
Analyse the need for a product by researching ingredients, environmental impact and ethical sourcing.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What is in a lip balm, and what does each ingredient do?
Conceptual: How do user appeal, sustainability and responsibility pull against each other in a product? How does knowing an ingredient's source change what we think of it?
Debatable: Should a product be judged on how well it works or on how it is made?
Process
Part of the MYP 1 unit Sustainable Glam (key concept: development; global context: globalization and sustainability). In Lesson 1 the cycle was slowed down: students worked with curated readings and were taught how to skim for relevance, scan for critical information and tell description from implication. They then turned their notes into running notes, graphic organisers, a hand-drawn mind map, sticky notes and a written reflection.
Skills and criteria
ATL research and thinking skills. Criterion A: Inquiring and Analysing. Read the reflection.
What students found
Students moved from what they know about products to analysing function, impact and sourcing, and began to link materials, markets and responsibility.
Next inquiry
How can what they found shape the specification for their own product in Criterion B?

Butterfly of prints

Rainbow colours printed with cut vegetables inside a drawn butterfly outline.

Grade 7 student, maths through art
Brief
Learn how cutting a solid at different angles changes its cross-section, ahead of the volume of prisms.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What shapes appear when a vegetable is cut straight across, and when it is cut at an angle?
Conceptual: How does the angle of a cut change the shape of the cross-section? How is a cross-section connected to the volume of a prism?
Debatable: Is a print a mathematical model or a piece of art, or both?
Process
Students first predicted the shapes, then cut vegetables at different angles, painted the cut faces and printed what they found inside a drawn outline. They compared prints with a partner and explained why two cuts gave different shapes.
Skills and criteria
ATL thinking skills (predicting, transfer) and communication skills (explaining a result). Mathematical links: cross-sections, 3D shapes and prisms.
What students found
The shape changed with the angle of the cut, and each picture was different, so each student's perspective showed.
Next inquiry
If a prism is a stack of identical cross-sections, how could we use the printed shape to find its volume?

Water saving system, simulated

A simulated Arduino system reads a pressure value and switches between a "peak" and a "normal" water mode, shown by red and green lights.

MYP Design student, product simulation
Inquiry, process and learning
Brief
Design and simulate a product that helps save water, and test that the logic works before building it.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What does the system measure, and what does it do when the reading is above or below the threshold?
Conceptual: How can a simple rule, such as a threshold, change how a resource is used? Why simulate before making?
Debatable: Is saving water better handled by a device or by changing habits?
What the student built
A potentiometer stands in for water pressure. The code reads it, prints the value, and sets the mode: above a threshold of 100 it reports "PEAK (35 sec)" with the red LED, otherwise "NORMAL (45 sec)" with the green LED. The serial monitor shows each reading and mode.
Process
The student wrote the code in Wokwi, wired the circuit, ran the simulation and turned the potentiometer to show both modes. Their own red circles and comments on the code explain the threshold, the "greater than" test and the "else" branch in plain words.
Skills and criteria
ATL communication and thinking skills. Criterion C (creating the solution) and Criterion D (evaluating).
What the student found
The same device behaves differently on either side of the threshold, and explaining the code line by line made the logic clear to others.
Next inquiry
Which threshold would suit a real tap, and how could the system be tested with a real sensor?

Relay module and timed lights, simulated

Water leakage sensor, simulated

A simulated Arduino with a pressure sensor takes a baseline reading, then raises a red light and a buzzer if the pressure changes enough to suggest a leak.

MYP Design student, product simulation
Inquiry, process and learning
Brief
Design a sensor system that detects a water leak early and alerts the user.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What does the pressure sensor measure, and what is a baseline reading?
Conceptual: How can comparing a live reading with a baseline show that something has changed? Why does the code read the pressure a second time to confirm before it raises the alarm?
Debatable: Is an alarm that sometimes gives a false warning better or worse than no alarm?
What the student built
A green LED shows the system is on. The sketch sets a baseline pressure at start-up. In the loop it reads the current pressure and, if it differs from the baseline by more than the threshold of 50, waits two seconds as a stability check and reads again. Only if the second reading also differs does it turn on the red LED and sound the buzzer at 1000 Hz; otherwise the red LED and buzzer stop.
Process
The student wired the circuit in Wokwi, wrote and annotated the code ("adjust after testing" on the threshold, comments on each buzzer line) and ran it. The recording is a phone video of the student working through the code on screen.
Skills and criteria
ATL critical-thinking and self-management skills. Criterion C (creating the solution) and Criterion D (evaluating).
What the student found
A single reading can be wrong; checking twice makes the alert more trustworthy. Marking the threshold "adjust after testing" shows the student expected to tune it with evidence.
Next inquiry
How would the threshold be chosen for a real pipe, and how could false alarms be reduced further?

Soil moisture monitor: a working prototype

A real Arduino circuit with a soil moisture sensor and a small display, tested in three pots labelled wet, medium and dry.

MYP Design student, physical prototype
Inquiry, process and learning
Brief
Build and test a device that tells a plant owner how moist the soil is.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What does the sensor measure, and what does the display show in wet, medium and dry soil?
Conceptual: How can a reading become a message a person understands? Why test in more than one condition?
Debatable: Should a plant be watered by a device that decides, or by a person who is informed?
What the student built
An Arduino with a two-pronged soil moisture sensor, a small OLED display and a breadboard of wires. Three pots are labelled WET, MEDIUM and DRY, and the sensor is moved between them to show how the reading and display change.
Process
The student wired the circuit, set up the three test conditions with labelled pots, and tested the sensor in each, wiping and re-testing between pots, while recording the run to share the results.
Skills and criteria
ATL critical thinking, research and self-management skills. Criterion C (creating the solution) and Criterion D (evaluating).
What the student found
Labelled test conditions turn a number into evidence: the device clearly separates wet, medium and dry soil.
Next inquiry
What reading counts as dry for different plants, and how could the device warn the owner automatically?

Brief
Design a digital product that helps a family manage screen time fairly.
Inquiry questions
Factual: What can a parent do on the dashboard, and how does completing a task change the balance?
Conceptual: How can a system make a trade-off, such as chores for screen time, visible and fair? What does a good user flow look like?
Debatable: Should screen time be earned, or should it be limited by rules alone?
What the student built
A "Parent Dashboard" with an available screen-time balance in minutes, a Task Manager where tasks such as "Clean room" and "Read 20min" are added with a time value and verified, and an Allocate Screen Time panel where minutes are assigned to an app. A flow strip shows Task, Verified, Reward, App time. There are also buttons to reset the day and undo the last verification, and a Guide and Info tab.
Process
The recording walks through the product: verifying a task raises the balance (5 to 25 minutes), assigning time lowers it, and the guide tab explains how to use the system.
Skills and criteria
ATL communication and social skills. Criterion C (creating the solution) and Criterion D (evaluating).
What the student found
A clear flow from task to verified to reward makes the rules of the system easy for a family to see and trust.
Next inquiry
How would children use it, and what feedback from users would change the design?
