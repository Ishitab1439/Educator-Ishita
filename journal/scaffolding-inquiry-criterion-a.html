Slowing the design cycle down: scaffolding inquiry in Criterion A

[Month Year] ClassroomArticle4-min read

The situation

As we entered Criterion A: Inquiring and Analysing, I noticed something both familiar and entirely natural. Students began with confidence drawn from lived experience. They knew products, preferences, advertisements and routines. However, when asked to consider ingredient function, environmental impact or ethical sourcing, many remained at an early stage of understanding.

This was not a weakness. It was a starting point. Young learners often approach design through what is visible and personal. Moving from familiarity to analysis requires explicit support in how knowledge is constructed.

What I tried

So in Lesson 1, I slowed the cycle down. Rather than advancing quickly towards ideas, I prioritised building the intellectual conditions that make ideation meaningful later.

Students worked with curated reading materials connected to everyday products. Alongside content, I explicitly taught ATL research and thinking skills: how to skim for relevance, scan for critical information, recognise consequence, and distinguish between description and implication. They then translated interpretation into structure using running notes, graphic organisers and eventually a hand-drawn mind map. The goal was not coverage. It was comprehension.

What happened

What began to shift was subtle: students started asking more precise questions, noticing tensions between user appeal and sustainability, and making early links between materials, markets and responsibility.

Although firmly positioned within Criterion A, this lesson was already influencing the rest of the design cycle. The clarity of specifications, strength of ideas and quality of justification in later phases depend heavily on how securely understanding is built at the outset.

What I learned

For me, this marked a change in professional stance. Inquiry is not something I can assume. It is something I must design for and teach.
