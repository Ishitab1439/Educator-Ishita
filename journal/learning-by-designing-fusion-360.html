Learning by designing: what Fusion 360 taught me about thinking

9 June 2025 MakingArticle4-min read

The situation

While exploring Fusion 360 for a personal design project, I stumbled into an unexpected learning experience. I was not preparing a lesson or trying to master a skill for the classroom. I was simply curious.

What I noticed

As I sketched, constrained, extruded and rotated objects in 3D space, I found myself deeply engaged in mathematical thinking, in ways that felt intuitive, purposeful and fun. That is when it struck me: why do we not teach Math like this? In that moment I was not just building a model.

  • I had to plan my sequence: which sketch to draw first and what constraints to apply.
  • I had to visualise in 3D, not just how something looks but how it behaves in space.
  • I had to problem-solve creatively: when the model did not behave as expected, I had to debug my logic, not just redo a step.

What happened

These are the Approaches to Learning (ATL) thinking skills we aim to nurture, especially thinking strategies and brainstorming strategies. They emerged organically through a technical tool I had not associated with core academic learning. Thinking strategies such as sequencing, predicting outcomes and reasoning through constraints are built into the computer-aided design (CAD) workflow. Brainstorming comes naturally: I found myself generating alternatives, testing hypotheses and reimagining designs, with constant reflection. Is this efficient? Could this be better? What if I tried this instead?

What I learned

Math in Fusion 360 is not about solving a problem. It is about constructing one and then solving it. You do not just calculate volume, you design an object and need to know its volume to make it work. You reason, apply, evaluate and create. That is Bloom's Taxonomy at its highest level, and it happens through modelling, not worksheets.

When I wrote this, I had not yet taught Fusion 360 to my students, but I felt compelled to. If one afternoon with the tool could do this for me, I wanted to see what it could do for them.
